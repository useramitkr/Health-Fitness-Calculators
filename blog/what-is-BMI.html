What is BMI and Why One Should Calculate It for Overall Health

By Amit Kr | Published on January 12, 2026

Obesity or overweight has now become a common problem among many individuals across the world. Moreover, obese people are often suffering from various other problems or are at the risk of diabetes, kidney problems, and heart diseases. If you are dealing with obesity problems, you must take steps to overcome it.

One of the best ways to deal with obesity is to calculate your BMI i.e. the Body Mass Index. Many research studies have revealed that people with overweight conditions often have a relatively high BMI. Indeed, calculating BMI is the first step to taking the necessary steps to reduce it.

What is Body Mass Index?

Body Mass Index or BMI refers to the body fat measurement according to the weight and height of an adult man or woman. The measurement considers only your weight and height but does not consider your bone density, muscle mass, or composition of your body.

How BMI is Useful for Healthcare Providers

Your healthcare provider may use BMI to diagnose the weight types and as one of the screening tools to diagnose specific health conditions. Accordingly, BMI ranges in kg per square meter will classify the following weight types:

  • BMI less than 18.5: Indicates underweight condition.
  • BMI between 18.5 and 24.9: Implies optimum body weight or healthy BMI.
  • BMI exceeding 25 but within 29.9: Indicates overweight.

Other than that, Class I obesity is from 30 to 34.9 while Class II obesity is from 35 to 39.9. Lastly, any person with BMI higher than 40 has Class III obesity.

Formula to Calculate the Body Mass Index (BMI)

Body Mass Index or BMI is calculated by measuring the body weight of an individual in kilograms divided by the square of his/her height in meters. Accordingly, the formula is:

BMI = Weight in Kilograms / (Height in Meters)²

Note: BMI does not measure your body fat directly. Instead, it is moderately associated with related body weight/fat measurements capturing the body fat location, amount, and its distribution.

Why to Calculate Body Mass Index

Calculation of Body Mass Index or BMI meets the following purposes:

1. Screening of Various Health Risks from BMI

Health Risks with Underweight or Low BMI Value

Underweight individuals, whose BMI is lesser than 18.5, are at a relatively high risk to develop the following diseases/health conditions:

  • Anemia
  • Malnutrition
  • Weak immune system
  • Osteoporosis
  • Infertility

People with underweight conditions must consult with their doctor to undergo specific blood tests and related tests to check their overall health to avoid malnutrition.

Health Risks with Overweight or High BMI Value

Overweight people or the ones with high BMI value are at the following health risks/conditions:

  • Hypertension or high blood pressure
  • Heart or cardiac diseases
  • Type 2 diabetes
  • Gallbladder stones
  • Sleep apnea
  • Osteoarthritis
  • Depression and related mental health conditions
  • Gallbladder, endometrial, breast, colon, and other types of cancers

2. Other Uses of BMI Calculation

Targeting the Right Body Weight: Your BMI value may assess your overweight or underweight condition. Moreover, you may use the optimal value of Body Mass Index for a specific height to make the necessary changes in your diet plan and lifestyle for targeting an optimal body weight. If this is not enough, many gyms and fitness centers use BMI calculation to track the members' weight gain or weight loss.

Customized Health Plan: The combination of your BMI results and medical history may help your doctor to design a customized health plan and thereby, help you achieve a healthy body weight within a specific period. The health plan will consist of precise information on your diet, lifestyle changes, exercise, and related parameters to achieve your overall health and well-being.

Give You Enough Confidence: An optimum BMI reading helps you elevate your spirit and boost your confidence levels, which further contribution to better life quality and overall health.

Additional Factors to Consider with the BMI Value

Many doctors consider a few additional factors along with the BMI value to determine the risks related to chronic diseases and thereby, give a complete picture of their patients’ health. The additional factors are as follows:

  • Medical history, which includes family history or existing health conditions.
  • Health behaviors, like sleep, physical activity, and diet.
  • Findings of a physical examination, like muscle mass and blood pressure.

Limitations of Body Mass Index

A major limitation of body mass index is that it fails to differentiate between the muscle and fat and overlooks fat distribution. However, there are a few other limitations as well:

Fails to Differentiate Between Body Fat and Muscles

High muscle mass may sometimes display a high BMI, especially in bodybuilders and athletes. As muscles are denser than body fat, BMI may label them incorrectly as overweight.

Overlooks the Distribution of Body Fat

BMI reading fails to show the exact area of the stored body fat. Remember that visceral or abdominal fat carries a relatively high risk of diabetes and cardiac problems as compared to the fat accumulated on thighs and hips.

Does not Consider the Individual’s Age and Gender

Muscle mass reduces gradually with age while the composition of body varies by gender. However, body mass index reading does not consider this aspect in its calculation.

Genetic and Ethnic Variations

The patterns of fat distribution and body compositions vary a lot based on diverse ethnic groups. In this situation, one cannot consider the single BMI standard as a universally accurate one.

Not Suitable for Every Population

BMI value is less reliable for children, pregnant women, old adults, and people going through specific medical conditions.

Conclusion

Overall, Body Mass Index is a vital indicator of an individual’s health. Many doctors use BMI value with other factors/metrics to assess the risks related to health conditions in their patients. Moreover, BMI value empowers individuals to formulate a plan to lead a healthy lifestyle and take the necessary proactive steps to reduce the underlying health risks.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is BMI and why is it important?

BMI (Body Mass Index) is a simple measurement that uses your height and weight to assess whether you fall into a healthy weight range. It helps identify potential health risks related to being underweight, overweight, or obese.

2. How is BMI calculated?

BMI is calculated by dividing a person’s weight in kilograms by the square of their height in meters (kg/m²). Many online BMI calculators make this process quick and easy without manual calculations.

3. What is a healthy BMI range?

A healthy BMI range for adults is typically 18.5 to 24.9. Below 18.5 is considered underweight, 25–29.9 is overweight, and 30 or above is classified as obese.

4. Is BMI accurate for everyone?

BMI is a useful general guideline but may not be accurate for athletes, pregnant women, older adults, or people with high muscle mass. In such cases, BMI should be considered alongside other health indicators.

5. Can BMI predict health risks?

BMI can indicate potential risks such as heart disease, diabetes, and high blood pressure. However, it does not measure body fat directly, so medical evaluation is recommended for a complete health assessment.
